Activity 1.2 – Diet and Energy Needs

1. Compare your diet with the natural diet of your organism. What are its primary food
sources?

My diet includes many different foods such as meat, fruits, vegetables, and snacks. A red panda
mainly eats bamboo, but it also eats fruit, berries, insects, eggs, and small animals.

2. Would your organism eat frequently throughout the day, graze continuously, or
consume large meals separated by long periods?

A red panda eats small amounts of food many times during the day because bamboo is low in
nutrients and energy.

3. What adaptations allow your organism to obtain enough energy in its environment?

Red pandas have strong jaws and teeth for bamboo chewing. They also have a special wrist bone
that acts like a thumb to help them grip bamboo while eating.

4. Predict one challenge your organism faces when food is scarce and explain how it adapts.

A challenge for red pandas is finding enough bamboo during harsh weather or when their habitat
is lost. They adapt by searching for larger areas for food and eating other foods such as fruits,
berries, insects, and eggs when available.

Activity 1.5 – Digestion and Enzymes

1. Does your organism produce lactase throughout life, only when young, or not at all?

Red pandas produce lactase when they are young so they can digest their mother's milk. As
adults, they produce much less lactase.

2. Identify one digestive enzyme that is especially important in your organism.
The original portfolio identifies cellulase as important because it helps break down the bamboo
that makes up most of a red panda's diet.

3. Explain how enzyme activity contributes to your organism's ecological niche.

Digestive enzymes help red pandas get nutrients and energy from bamboo. This allows them to
live in forests where bamboo is their main food source.

Activities 1.4 and 1.6 – ATP and Metabolism

1. How does your organism obtain the ATP needed for movement, growth, reproduction,
or survival?

A red panda gets ATP by eating bamboo, fruits, berries, insects, and other foods. Its cells break
down these nutrients to produce energy.

2. Describe one situation where energy demand increases dramatically.

A red panda needs more energy when climbing trees or searching for food.

3. Predict which biomolecule serves as its primary long-term energy source.

Fats are the primary long-term energy source because they store energy for later use.

4. Explain one metabolic adaptation that helps your organism survive in its environment.

A red panda has a slow metabolism, which helps it save energy while eating a low-energy diet of
bamboo.

5. Identify one behavior that requires major metabolic changes.

Surviving during cold winter months requires metabolic changes because the red panda must
conserve energy and stay warm.

A red panda uses cell division to grow from a tiny cub into an adult. New cells are made as its

A red panda can repair damaged tissues and heal wounds, but it cannot regrow entire body parts.

3. Which tissues divide most rapidly?

4. When would rapid cell division be useful, and why must it be regulated?

Activity 3.2 – Adaptation and Reproduction

1. Describe one adaptation of your organism and identify a selective pressure that may
have favored it.

The red panda has thick fur that helps keep it warm in cold mountain forests. Cold weather is the
selective pressure that may have favored this adaptation.

2. Investigate how your organism reproduces.
Red pandas reproduce sexually. A male and female mate, and the female gives birth to cubs. The
mother takes care of the cubs until they are old enough to live on their own.

3. Explain how offspring inherited genetic information from their parents.

The cubs get half of their genes from their mother and half from their father. This makes each
cub a little different.

4. Predict what might happen to the adaptation if the environment changed. Is your
organism still evolving?

If the environment became warmer, thick fur might not be as useful. Over time, red pandas with
traits better suited for warmer weather may survive more often. Red pandas are still evolving
because natural selection continues to happen.

Activity 3.3 – Natural Selection

1. Imagine your organism in the simulation.

My organism is a red panda that lives in forests with lots of trees and bamboo. Red pandas use
their claws to climb trees, find food, and stay safe from predators. Their red and brown fur helps
them blend in with trees and branches. They need food, shelter, and protection to survive.

2. What mutations could appear?

Possible mutations include:

   •   Lighter or darker fur.
   •   Longer or shorter tails.
   •   Stronger or weaker claws.
   •   Better or poorer eyesight.

3. Which mutations increase or decrease fitness?

Some mutations can help red pandas survive. Stronger claws can help them climb trees and
escape predators. Fur that blends in with the trees can help them hide. Better eyesight can help
them find food and see predators.

Weak claws or poor eyesight can make survival harder and make it more difficult to find food or
avoid danger.

4. Provide a narrative describing how selection could change the organism over
generations.
First, red pandas have different traits. Some have stronger claws, while others have weaker
claws. Red pandas with stronger claws may be better at climbing trees and escaping predators.
They may have a better chance of surviving and having babies, and they can pass helpful traits to
their young. Over many generations, those traits may become more common. This is natural
selection.

Activity 3.4 – Biomes and Habitat

1. Locate your organism's biome.

Red pandas live in the temperate forest biome. They are found in the Himalayan mountains and
parts of China, Nepal, India, Bhutan, and Myanmar. They live in high mountain forests with
trees and bamboo.

2. Record climate and habitat characteristics.

Red pandas live in cool, wet mountain forests. These forests have:

   •   Cool temperatures.
   •   Plenty of rain and moisture.
   •   Tall trees and thick plants.
   •   Lots of bamboo for food.
   •   Trees and branches where red pandas can climb and sleep.

Red pandas have thick fur to keep warm and long, fluffy tails that help them stay warm and
balance while climbing.

3. Compare with a biome where it is not likely to thrive or may not even be able to survive.

A desert would be difficult for red pandas. Deserts are very hot and dry, with little rain and few
trees. Red pandas need cool forests, water, and bamboo. A desert would not provide enough
food, shade, or shelter.

4. Explain why your organism cannot easily live in other biomes.

Red pandas cannot easily live in deserts or grasslands because these places may not have enough
bamboo or trees. They need trees for climbing and protection. Their thick fur also makes very
hot places difficult for them.

5. Describe how humans have altered their habitat.

Humans have changed red pandas' homes by cutting down trees, building roads, and clearing
forests for farms. This destroys places where red pandas live and find food. Hunting and capture
are also threats. When forests are destroyed, red pandas have less bamboo and fewer safe places
to live.

Activity 3.5 – Habitat Through Time

1. What was the landscape like in 1700 compared with 2000?

The original portfolio describes much of the red panda range in 1700 as likely less changed by
humans, with more natural forests. By 2000, more land had been used for farms, villages, roads,
and other human activities. These changes were reduced and divided into forests.

2. Identify two human-caused changes and the activities responsible.

Two important changes are:

   •   Cutting down trees to make farms, collect wood, and build homes.
   •   Building roads and expanding farms, which break forests into smaller areas and make it
       harder for red pandas to move safely.

3. Were these changes beneficial or harmful? Explain.

These changes are mostly harmful to red pandas. They lose places to sleep, climb, and hide, as
well as bamboo for food. Roads can separate groups and make it harder for red pandas to find
mates.

4. Identify an adaptation that may help and explain its limits.

A long, bushy tail helps red pandas balance while climbing and staying warm. Sharp claws also
help them climb trees. However, these adaptations cannot replace a lost forest or provide food
when bamboo is gone.

5. Predict a future ecological challenge.

If forest cutting and road building continue, red pandas may lose more habitat, food, and hiding
places. Their population could become smaller. Protecting forests can help them survive.

Activity 3.6 – Tree of Life

1. Locate your organism on the Tree of Life.

The red panda's scientific name is Ailurus fulgens. It belongs to the animal kingdom, is a
mammal, and is in the order of Carnivora. It is in its own family, Ailurid. Red pandas are the
only living members of this family.
2. Identify some close relatives and a common ancestor.

The original portfolio identifies raccoons, weasels, otters, and skunks as relatives. These animals
share an ancient common ancestor with red pandas.

3. Compare one close and one distant relative.

A raccoon is a close relative. Both red pandas and raccoons are mammals, have claws, and can
climb trees. However, raccoons have different body shapes and black face markings.

A giant panda is a more distant relative. Both eat bamboo, but giant pandas belong to the bear
family, and red pandas belong to the Ailurid family.

4. Identify one ancestral and one recently evolved trait.

   •   Ancestral trait: Sharp claws help red pandas climb and were also present in their
       ancestors.
   •   Recently evolved trait: The red panda has a false thumb, which is an extra wrist bone
       that helps it hold bamboo while eating.

Activity 4.1 – Population Ecology

1. Describe the population pattern. Is it increasing, decreasing, stable, or uncertain?

The original portfolio reports that the red panda population is decreasing and gives an estimate of
fewer than 10,000 in the wild. It describes the species as endangered. Counting red pandas is
difficult because they live in high mountain forests and spend a lot of time in trees.

2. Identify two limiting factors and explain whether they are top-down or bottom-up.

Factor 1: Lack of bamboo (bottom-up factor)

Red pandas eat mostly bamboo. When forests are cut down, there may be less bamboo available.
Without enough food, red pandas may have trouble surviving and being young. This is a bottom-
up factor because it starts with the food supply.

Factor 2: Predators and disease (top-down factor)

The original portfolio notes that wild predators and free-roaming dogs can threaten red pandas.
Dogs can also spread disease. These dangers can cause red pandas to die and make their
population smaller. This is a top-down factor because it involves dangers from other animals and
diseases.
3. Choose one limiting factor and explain whether it is density-dependent or density-
independent.

Food competition can be density dependent. When more red pandas live in one area, they may
have to share limited bamboo. If there is not enough food, some may have trouble surviving and
reproducing.

4. Explain carrying capacity.

The carrying capacity depends on available food, water, and forest space. Bamboo, large trees,
and safe places to live are important. If forests are lost, the habitat can support fewer red pandas.

Activity 4.2 – Food Web and Ecological Relationships

1. Describe the trophic role and energy flow.

Red pandas are mostly plant eaters. They get most of their energy from bamboo leaves and
shoots. Bamboo gets energy from sunlight.

The energy pathway in the original portfolio is:

Sun → Bamboo → Red Panda → Snow Leopard

   •   Sun: Provides energy.
   •   Bamboo: Producer because it makes its own food.
   •   Red panda: Primary consumer because it eats plants.
   •   Snow leopard: Listed in the portfolio as a possible predator.

2. Identify at least two organisms that interact directly with your organism.

Bamboo – Food source

Red pandas eat bamboo leaves and shoots. They depend on bamboo for most of their food. If
bamboo becomes harder to find, red pandas may struggle to survive.

Snow leopard – Possible predator

The original portfolio lists snow leopards as possible as predators. Red pandas use their sharp
claws to climb trees and escape danger.

Livestock – Competitor

Animals such as cattle and goats can compete for vegetation and bamboo. When livestock eat too
much vegetation, red pandas may have less food available.
3. If your organism's population increased or decreased, which other organisms would be
affected first?

If red pandas decrease, they may eat less bamboo, so bamboo could increase in some places. The
original portfolio suggests that predators could also lose one possible food source, although the
effect may be small because red pandas are not a major food source for snow leopards.

4. Predict one indirect effect elsewhere in the food web.

If fewer red pandas feed on bamboo, bamboo may grow more in some areas. If it becomes very
thick, it could block sunlight from smaller plants below it, making it harder for those plants to
grow.

5. Explain the keystone connection.

The original portfolio does not identify red pandas as a confirmed keystone species. It describes
them as an umbrella species. This means protecting their habitat can also help protect other
plants and animals from living there.
